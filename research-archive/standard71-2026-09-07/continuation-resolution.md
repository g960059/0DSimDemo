## Finding and controls

The original failure at `0d86a702` was real but did not establish a physiological
problem. Standard71 wraps a physical-state checkpoint that omits the coupled
Newton predictor. At an ordinary lean boundary the source has history depth4;
restore starts with depth0. A finite-tolerance Newton solve can accept slightly
different floating-point roots from different initial guesses.

The historical base tests distinguish a physical-state restart, which continues
scientifically, from a binary checkpoint with bit-identical continuation.
Standard71's single unconditional `toEqual` incorrectly assumed the stronger
contract for the former. More importantly, the local artifact qualification
captured immediately after TBV control, when the new Session's history was
empty, and compared only one resumed step. It therefore missed ordinary warm
continuation. That old passing report is preserved, not reclassified as proof
of arbitrary warm continuation.

The [one-second readback](checkpoint-restart-one-second-v1.json), generated by
[this diagnostic](checkpoint-restart-audit.ts), extends the control to cold and
settled starts, five2ms warmup steps, then5002ms comparisons of three Sessions:
one uninterrupted source and two independent restores of the same checkpoint.
The numerical source was unchanged during these measurements.

The cold diagnostic has not accumulated the same complete-beat information as

later beat would differ. In the settled case the LV positive dP/dt difference
was about3.43e−8mmHg/s. These are numerical continuation differences, not evidence
for changing the physiological baseline or tolerances.

The artifact verifier now warms eight steps after control and requires1000

## Adopted core design

An independent Codex6 Astra max review supported one durable checkpoint that
stores the existing physical checkpoint plus the existing predictor V2. It is
numerical continuation state, not an added physiological state or a new force,
valve, circulation, or integration law. Workspaces and diagnostic counters are
not added to persistence. The current identity policy requires a distinct exact
model identity for the changed continuation contract: the unregistered
Standard72 candidate reuses the unchanged Standard71 physical fixture.

Both branches are detached at one accepted epoch before asynchronous hashing.
Restore checks strict field/schema identity, digest, owner clocks and the
predictor's current root against the accepted state. It reuses the existing
root representation validator without widening its tolerance. Missing history
is rejected; an actually empty canonical history remains valid.71 envelopes are
not automatically upgraded or relabeled. Legacy checkpoint APIs cannot silently
drop72 history. Capture has no effect on the live solver's predictor or result.

The old71 test now checks exact initial physical state, successful deterministic
restarts and incompatible model/fixture rejection. It no longer claims
uninterrupted bit equality. That stronger requirement is explicitly tested in
the successor; it is not replaced with a wider floating-point tolerance.
