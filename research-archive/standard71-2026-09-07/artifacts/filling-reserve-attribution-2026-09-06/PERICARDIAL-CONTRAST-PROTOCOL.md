# Bounded external-constraint contrast, after the LA/Ca factorial

The factorial showed zero external LV pressure throughout the center/low
readback beats, but a maximum of 3.84 mmHg at high TBV in the selected anchor.
At mitral closure, high-load pressure includes approximately 3.26 mmHg of
external pressure. These are trajectory accounts, not a causal CO effect.

Before running further fitting, compare the existing exact pericardial
reference-capacity scales 0.95 and 1.05 against the completed scale-1 anchor.
Retain its Ca trough 0.164321 uM, LA active scale 1 and every other input.
Use two independent cold 2ms workers, formal fixed-tone low/center/high TBV
and the same endpoint phase readback. No pressure-scale or stiffness change,
no added fluid, no pericardium-off case, no afterload challenge or gate change.
This is a mechanism intervention, not an admitted baseline-fitting coordinate.

Predeclare no adoption from a higher reserve/pass count. Check whether the
constraint engages differently at center; quantify PCWP versus transmural
pressure, LV/RV volume redistribution, cap exposure and actual CO response.
A larger reference capacity may allow a larger EDV without an improved pump;
it is not automatically a healthier construction. Preserve failures too.

The canonical pericardium is an anatomy-based structural prior, explicitly
not a pressure-fitting knob. Five percent is a local sensitivity contrast,
not a literature-derived normal interval. Even a strong effect would require
an independent anatomy/loading justification before changing that prior.

Relevant primary evidence illustrates both the mechanism and transfer limits:

- Applegate et al., 1992, eight open-chest anesthetized dogs with micromanometer
  pressure and conductance volume, found little constraint at low volumes and
  increasing constraint after volume loading. Not a human threshold for this
  fixed-control TBV protocol. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1621831/
- Mangano et al., 1985, 15 coronary-surgery patients before/after pericardiotomy,
  used radionuclide LV volume and PCWP with position changes. They found no
  significant curve changes with normal/moderately elevated filling. Not
  healthy volunteers or proof that all normal hearts have no constraint.
  https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/S0735-1097%2885%2980162-5

These studies do not identify the correct reference capacity or justify a
required high-side reserve percentage for the present model.
