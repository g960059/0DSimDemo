Decision: **CONDITIONAL APPROVE** for the concrete LVFW+SEP active-amplitude range and workbench exposure described below. A first development preset at LVFW=SEP=0.35, with every other input at the Standard72 baseline, is scientifically adequate **as an explicitly limited LV systolic-dysfunction demonstration**, subject to the remaining acceptance conditions. ## Scientific assessment

The proposed change has a clear model meaning: it multiplies Land `Tref` in the LV free wall and the shared septum. In `MainWireNormalAdultFiveWallProviderV1.ts`, active scaling changes the amplitude parameter while leaving the kinetic parameters intact. The Standard71/72 constitutive baseline is itself calibrated, including a ventricular `Tref` of approximately 238.8 kPa; consequently 0.35 is a multiplier of this model reference, not a measured patient contractility, 35% viable myocardium, or 35% of a physiological universal maximum.

This is an appropriate first intervention for a mechanistic demonstration. Reduced active stress at unchanged calcium drive, passive law, structural reference geometry, and circulation inputs produces lower ejection and work, higher LV filling pressure, and chamber distension. A reference geometry can remain fixed while the simulated cavity volume changes. Adding a remodeling law or distorting passive mechanics solely to cross the preferred EDVI=100 endpoint would introduce more assumptions than the present objective justifies.

The reference appropriately distinguishes cohort statistics, broad construction screens, and preferred construction targets. Those source populations do not define a simultaneous standard patient. EF, EDVI, and CI are algebraically coupled at fixed HR and no regurgitation, so their agreement is useful consistency evidence but is not three independent validations of mechanism. The current ranking acknowledges that dependence. The limited current-domain search supports extending the domain; it does not prove that all other current-domain settings are incapable of reduced EF.

The 0.35 candidate is preferable to the search winner for the first development preset. It changes only one grouped operation, is on the existing numeric lattice, has useful margin from the disease screen boundaries, and already has independent cold/fine evidence. The winner changes loading inputs as well, misses more preferred targets, and is not representable by the present workbench controls. There is no scientific need to preserve the winner's rank as a product requirement.

## Material limitations and risks

**Ventricular interaction is part of this intervention.** The septum is shared. The candidate's RVEF falls to about 43% and mean PAP rises from 17.89 to 27.95 mmHg while RVFW amplitude remains 1. This does not establish primary RV muscle disease; it shows that “RV input unchanged” cannot be presented as “RV physiology unchanged.” Display the shared-septum scope beside the LV control. Likewise, unchanged resistance and TBV are matched *inputs*, not matched physiological preload and afterload: LA and aortic pressures change substantially in the closed loop. This comparison is not a load-independent Ees measurement.

**The timing response is not a general HFrEF teaching model.** From baseline to 0.35, native ICT shortens from about 89 to 42 ms, ET lengthens from 258 to 286 ms, and Tei falls from 0.702 to 0.483. These are major directional changes even though the final values may individually look plausible. Altered filling and ejection pressure conditions and native event conventions can affect these measures; this review does not establish their complete causal decomposition. Patel et al. report shorter SET and longer PEP in their HFrEF cohort, and PEP is not native ICT. Do not describe the present Tei change as improving ventricular health or present these timing changes as universal HFrEF behavior. Retain the outputs with this limitation rather than tune calcium or hide timing to improve agreement. [Patel et al., full article, Introduction and Table 2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ejhf.1659).

**Relaxation context is partly unresolved.** At 0.35, the Weiss result is about 50.4 ms (49.9 ms at 1 ms), with a monotone decline and small endpoint sensitivity. However, its fitting window is only about 20.6 ms, ending against a high next-filling pressure. The free-asymptote calculation returns roughly 148 ms with an asymptote near −73 mmHg and fails the method's quality classification. The published `glantzTauMs` correctly remains null. Do not promote the internal fit number into a validated tau, compare it directly with a source's nonzero-asymptote tau, or interpret the change in Weiss tau as evidence that calcium kinetics were slowed. Preserve the fit quality and unavailable reason.

**One source has an internal discrepancy.** The registry's Patel relaxation value, 93.3 [67.3, 122] ms, does match the HFrEF column of Table 2. The abstract instead assigns the 78.7 ms median to HFrEF and 93.3 ms to HFpEF. I independently verified both in the publisher HTML after initially flagging a possible transcription error. Keep the table-based provenance, add an explicit within-source inconsistency note, and keep the quantity contextual. Do not silently replace the number or modify an old search artifact/hash. A metadata correction can be versioned and the unchanged numerical decisions reassessed without rerunning a fitting batch. [Patel et al., Abstract versus Table 2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ejhf.1659).

**The sampled domain is not a physiological Cartesian guarantee.** The research sweep contains successful numerical evaluations at 0.25, but some fail the disease screen; at baseline TBV/Rsys, 0.25 gives mean LA about 25.15 mmHg. Most evidence concerns equal LVFW/SEP values, not independent extremes or RV amplitude reduction. The lower bound is a bounded engineering exploration choice. Acceptance must continue to distinguish legal input, successful numerical evolution, periodicity, and disease-screen qualification.

## Numerical evidence and remaining reachability gap

The 0.35 cold/fine agreement is strong for this purpose: the EF difference is approximately 0.021 percentage points, CI differs by 0.085%, EDVI by 0.022 mL/m², and mean aortic pressure by 0.053 mmHg. Native event quantities are less smooth: ET differs by 3 ms, ICT by 2 ms, and Tei by about 0.012. RV ICT differs by 6 ms. Do not demand bitwise or sub-step identity from event timings. Preserve their observed resolution rather than rounding away discrepancies.

The raw diagnostics show one forward episode/one significant ventricular pressure peak, ordered native closures, positive chamber volumes, nonnegative valve flows, smooth PV loops, and no detected relaxation dip/recovery. Maximum recorded global volume error is about 2.7e−12 mL and coronary ledger residual is below 2.5e−10 mL across these diagnostic runs. The plotted 1 and 2 ms PV/pressure curves nearly overlap. These checks support the candidate without establishing every input combination or every derived analysis method.

The independent cold disease runs required 210 cycles. The final periodicity residual is dominated by coronary tone, not calcium drift. This matters for workbench waiting/status and for deciding when captured values can be called settled; a short fixed beat count is insufficient.

Crucially, the saved warm diagnostic starts at **LVFW=SEP=0.30**, not at the healthy baseline of 1. Its agreement with the cold 0.35 endpoint supports local continuation but is not the requested baseline-to-preset route. The baseline cold run is a useful matched-input restored-amplitude comparator, but it is not a tested 0.35-to-1 runtime reversal. Those two routes remain to be verified through the actual workbench adapter.

## Smallest preferable implementation

Reuse the existing multi-wall operation pattern in `MainWireIntegratedStudioRoundedEjectionControlsV1.ts`: one registered operation assigns both LVFW and SEP, one reducer returns a two-path patch, and one fixture projection returns the common value or `mixed`. The workbench adapter already performs atomic warm fixture replacement with an input-epoch check for the existing global ventricular operation. Apply the LV group in one transaction and one invalidation. Do not invoke the two wall controls sequentially, because that temporarily creates a different mechanical state and can race captured results.

The exact layer must own validation and the atomic input operation; the Surface owns whether it is exposed, its presentation and default placement. A compound input operation is already a supported convention here. It adds no new physical scalar or checkpoint state: persist the two wall amplitudes and derive the group value from them. This is an acceptable small ownership tradeoff for this repository. If an equally small Surface affine mapping can use the existing atomic patch path, that is also acceptable, but there is no need to activate a general knob/protocol framework for this one operation. The schema's existence alone does not make a Surface knob executable.

Keep individual controls available for advanced use and replace LVFW-only active tension in the default pane with the LV group. Show `mixed` for unequal values, including after individual wall edits and capture/reload; never average them or overwrite one implicitly during readback. Setting the group intentionally assigns both values and preserves RVFW, both atria, passive/Ca inputs, and every other fixture field. Label it “LV収縮性” only with nearby “LV自由壁＋共有中隔の能動張力倍率” wording and an explanation of the multiplier.

## Acceptance conditions for this bundled approval

1. **Exact range scope:** LVFW and SEP active amplitude may be 0.25–1.33. RVFW and both atria remain 0.75–1.33. The common LV+SEP+RV control remains within the intersection, 0.75–1.33. Exact inputs remain continuous; the exposed numeric lattice remains 0.01. Do not introduce calcium, passive, geometry, valve, baseline, or solver changes into this bundle. The original experiment's all-ventricular 0.25 validator is not the approved final scope; the draft inspected at the end of this review correctly narrows it.

2. **Identity isolation:** create a distinct research exact release/checkpoint binding for the approved domain. Changing the shared range constant while retaining released72's identity is insufficient. Show that released72's source/runtime/artifact entry still rejects values it previously rejected, and that foreign checkpoints cannot enter the new owner. Carry the latest compatible production Surface and all pinned analysis methods forward explicitly; an exposure change needs its own Surface identity. Do not add analysis result placeholders to exact frames or persist a third disease/analysis execution identity as a substitute for the exact/Surface pair. Research provenance can separately pin source, reference, and input hashes.

3. **Actual control-route equivalence:** starting from the new research model's baseline, use the same registered workbench operation to set 0.35, capture/export, reload, and compare the canonical normalized fixture and fixture hash with the preset fixture exactly. Verify 0.35→1 through that adapter, and equality with the original baseline fixture. Separately verify settled output concordance against the saved cold endpoint and baseline. Checkpoint hashes need not match across different histories; fixture equality is exact, while trajectory endpoints require stated numerical tolerances. All non-LV-group inputs must compare unchanged, and the global group must read `mixed` at the disease point.

4. **Transactional and boundary behavior:** cover group endpoints, both individual lower-bound edits at baseline values of the other wall, mixed readback, repeated/same-value assignment, invalid/NaN/off-lattice input, capture/reload, and stale-epoch rejection. Add bounded numerical checks for the newly exposed lower endpoint and the actual baseline→0.35→1 transition, including abrupt versus intermediate group changes. They must evolve successfully or report a structured numerical failure without committing partial inputs, corrupting the previous accepted owner, or retaining stale results as current. This does not require an exhaustive Cartesian sweep or a new optimizer.

5. **Candidate qualification:** retain the 0.35 cold/fine results, verify the actual baseline/reversal warm routes with the existing periodicity/conservation policy, and record comparison tolerances. Reasonable acceptance targets for these major rest outputs are EF within 0.5 percentage points, indexed volumes and CI within 1%, and mean pressures within 0.5 mmHg; evaluate event-derived timings at their step resolution, separately. These are numerical comparison tolerances, not clinical thresholds. Do not loosen the healthy gate, disease screen, or preferred targets to obtain a pass.

6. **Surface and analysis continuity:** verify that default controls, PV overlays, latest production outputs, and pinned analysis methods remain available on baseline and candidate. Derived calculations that are inapplicable or cannot complete at a low-amplitude state must return their explicit unavailable status/reason, with cache invalidation keyed to the changed fixture/epoch. Do not silently substitute an older method or continue to show baseline results after applying the disease input. A new EF screen pass does not qualify formal ESPVR/PVA or healthy-reference outputs.

7. **Preset/reference presentation:** call this a source-informed LV systolic-dysfunction demonstration; record the EDVI target miss, altered RV physiology, timing direction caveat, and unresolved free-asymptote relaxation result. Add the Patel table/abstract inconsistency annotation with reproducible reference provenance. No normal-range, viability, isolated measured Ees, AMI, remodeled chronic HFrEF, or universal HFrEF claim is approved.

These are concrete completion checks within this review round, not a request for another paid approval merely because the implementation is finished. If they pass, I agree to the scoped range/exposure adoption and this first development preset. If the actual control transition fails, identity isolation cannot be maintained, or materially different model mechanisms are required, the failed condition must be resolved before adopting this candidate; this approval does not extend to that changed scope.
